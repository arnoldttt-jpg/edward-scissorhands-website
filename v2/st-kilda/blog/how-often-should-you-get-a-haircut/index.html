How often should you really get a haircut?

How often to get a haircut, by cut: skin fades every 2–3 weeks, tapers 3–4, scissor cuts 4–6. Signs you're due, and how to make a cut last longer.

There's no single answer. It depends on how short you go and how sharp you like it — here's how barbers think about it.

How often should you get a haircut?

The short answer: the shorter your cut, the more often you'll need it. For most people, hair grows about one to one and a half centimetres a month. On a longer scissor cut, an extra centimetre barely shows. On a skin fade, where the bottom is shaved to nothing, it's the difference between sharp and grown out.

As a guide, skin fades look best on a two to three week cycle, zero fades and tapers at three to four weeks, short scissor cuts at four to six, and longer styles at six to ten. Beards are their own thing: a quick tidy every week or two keeps the shape, even if you only have a full trim once a month.

What are the signs it's time to book back in?

  • The neckline has gone fuzzy and crept below where it was cut.
  • Your ears are starting to disappear under the sides.
  • The fade has lost its gradient and looks like one solid block.
  • Your hair won't sit the way it did, even with the same product.
  • Cowlicks and crowns have started doing their own thing again.

If two or more of these sound familiar, you're probably a week or so overdue.

Can you make a haircut last longer?

A little. Ask for a slightly longer bottom on your fade, such as a zero instead of skin or a No. 1 instead of a zero, and it will grow out more gradually.

Use the right product for your hair. On a short cut, a matte clay or paste hides early regrowth better than anything with shine — ask us, or browse the products we use in the shop. Wash with a gentle shampoo and don't overdo it, so your hair keeps some natural weight and sits flat.

And the simplest tip of all: book your next cut before you leave. It's much easier to keep a cut looking good on schedule than to rescue it after six weeks.

Does getting regular haircuts make your hair grow faster?

No — that one's a myth. Hair grows from the follicle under the skin, and cutting the ends has no effect on how fast it grows. What regular trims do is remove split and damaged ends, especially on longer hair, so it looks thicker and healthier as it grows.

If you're growing your hair out, a light trim every eight to ten weeks keeps the shape heading in the right direction while you wait. Still deciding how short to go? Our skin fade vs zero fade guide explains the options.
